[Verse 1 — measured, spoken, violins tracing a mocking minuet]
Upon our reacquaintance, sir, I shall be plain:
I care not for you. I care not for you.
Not for your enthusiasms, not your trials sustained,
Not the harbour where you mean to remain.

[Verse 2 — a shade quicker]
The gentleman who trembles at your tread — I care not.
Your turn at Her Majesty's pleasure — I care not.
Your fellowship, your parish, the confederacy you've got,
The men upon whom you have drawn — I care not.

[Verse 3 — quicker, pressing]
Your person and your lady — I care not for you.
The Continent you travelled — I care not for you.
That counterfeit cold scowl, the gilt between your teeth —
I care not for you. I care not for you.

[Verse 4 — faster, colour rising]
Hang your existence, hang your honest trade.
Hang the parish and the acquaintance you have made.
Hang the alms you scatter. Hang the eyes upon your back.
Hang the forked lies on your tongue — I care not.

[Bridge — rapid-fire, breathless]
Your shadowy confederates, the soil from which you came,
The manner of your fortune, the whole wretched frame,
Your forthcoming publication, your designs, your crew,
The part you fancy you play, the house rules you hold true,
I'll not be acquainted, sir, with you,
You fraudulent dandified wretch — damn it, damn it, damn it —

[Break — one held dissonant chord, then silence]

[Verse 5 — slowly, gravely, deliberate]
And a pox upon these harlots here present —
if they are not, at this very moment,
engaged in the act of fornication.

[Exchange — mezzo-soprano interjects, offended]
You wretch! Who are you, sir, to call us harlots?
I address every harlot in the room, you hussy.
Whom do you name a hussy?
Every hussy. Every harlot. The lot of you, here, now —

[Outro — riot, strings scrape to chaos]
Scoundrel! The constables! Scoundrel!
(A chair topples. Glass. A final violin shriek.)
Curtain.
